Purchase order rows, redesigned

Three ways to put the orderer, the order date, the linked estimate, the company, the items and the total on one line — built with real rows from your database.

33/34

Almost none of your POs are linked to an estimate. Only PO-000012 has one — which means “what estimate it’s attached to” and “the company it was ordered for” are both blank on 33 of 34 rows today.

The company name isn’t stored on a PO at all; it’s reached through the estimate. So a design that just adds two columns leaves most of your list looking empty. Every option below treats the unlinked state as the common case and turns that gap into a one-click Attach estimate action instead of dead space.

Option A

Ledger

Selected — building this

One line per PO, columns locked so your eye can run straight down any field. Items live behind a hover peek — the “floating list” you described. It fits the most rows on screen. Known trade-off: the item list needs a mouse, so it isn’t reachable on a tablet — the item count stays visible either way, and the peek is also keyboard-focusable.

What I'm building

  1. Option A rows. One dense line per PO carrying all five new fields plus a real total.
  2. Items in a hover peek off the item count, as you asked. Keyboard-focusable too, so it isn’t mouse-only for anyone tabbing through.
  3. Ordered-by as initials + name, with the exact date and time — and the stamp switched from Updated to Ordered. That’s a different field from the one on screen today.
  4. Unlinked POs get an Attach estimate button where the estimate and company would be. That fills 33 rows of empty space with the one action that makes the column useful — and every PO you attach starts showing a company name.
  5. “Subtotal” relabelled “Total.” POs carry no tax in your data, so the subtotal already is the total — the current label just undersells it.

Roughly a half-day. The query needs three additions — who created it, when, and the item lines — all of which already exist in the database.
